HVAC After Hours Answering Service: A Setup Guide

An after hours answering service picks up your phone when the office is closed and either books the job or pages your on-call tech. For HVAC owners, that means a no-heat call at midnight in January reaches a person instead of voicemail. This guide covers how to structure the service, what it costs, and what to hand your vendor on day one.

What the service handles and what to set up first

An answering service has three levels of involvement. Pick the one that fits your operation:

  1. Message only. The agent takes the caller's name, number, and problem, then texts or emails it to you. You call back in the morning.
  2. Dispatch. The agent applies your emergency rules and pages your on-call tech directly for qualifying calls. Everything else goes to the next-day queue.
  3. Direct booking. The agent books the appointment straight into your scheduling software while the caller is still on the line.

For HVAC, dispatch at minimum is the right level. A message-only service just documents what you missed. Before you call any vendor, write down your four call types and what happens to each:

  • No heat with outdoor temps below freezing: emergency, page on-call tech now
  • No cool with dangerous indoor temp or a vulnerable person in the home: emergency, page on-call tech now
  • System running poorly but still functional: next morning queue, book first available slot
  • Tune-up request or maintenance plan renewal: book into the schedule while the caller is on the line

If a vendor cannot explain how they handle each of these before you sign, they are not ready for HVAC dispatch.

What it costs: pricing models and hidden fees

For a small HVAC shop taking 30 to 80 after-hours calls per month, answeringservicecost.com reports typical monthly costs of $75 to $300. Costs rise with call volume, live-agent services, and peak-season surcharges.

Hidden costs to confirm before you sign:

  • Holiday surcharges: answeringservicecost.com reports some providers charge 1.5 to 2 times the normal rate on federal holidays.
  • Night and weekend premiums: some add 10 to 30 percent above base rates; many others charge nothing extra.
  • Per-dispatch fees: a separate charge each time the agent pages your on-call tech.
  • Monthly minimums: you pay the minimum even in a slow month.
  • Minute rounding: some services round up to the nearest full minute, turning a 90-second call into two billable minutes.

These prices change. Get a written rate sheet before you start and confirm holiday and weekend surcharge rules in writing.

Emergency triage script: copy and send this to your vendor

Print this, fill in your company name and emergency rules, and email it to any vendor before they go live. If they cannot work from a written script, do not use them.

Example

After-hours triage script for HVAC companies

Opening: "Thank you for calling [Company Name]. I am [Agent Name] on after-hours support. Is this a heating or cooling emergency?"

Step 1. Get the basics. "Can I get your name and best callback number? And the service address?"

Step 2. Identify the problem. "What is happening with the system right now?"

Step 3. Apply your emergency test.

  • No heat: "What is the indoor temperature right now? Is anyone in the home who is elderly, very young, or has a medical condition?"
  • No cool: "What is the indoor temperature? Is anyone in the home medically vulnerable to heat?"
  • Water leaking from the unit: treat as emergency automatically.
  • Gas smell or smoke: tell the caller to leave the building and call 911 immediately. Do not dispatch a technician.

Step 4. Route the call.

  • Emergency condition met: "I am reaching your on-call technician right now. They will call you at [number] within 20 minutes."
  • No emergency condition: "I will get this on the schedule for first thing tomorrow. You will receive a confirmation text to [number]."

Step 5. Close. "Is there anything else you need tonight?"

Questions to ask any vendor before you sign

A vendor that cannot answer these clearly is not ready for HVAC emergency dispatch.

  • Do your agents answer within 20 seconds? Callers with no heat hang up fast.
  • Do you answer in my company name or as a third-party service?
  • Can you follow a written triage script I provide, including my emergency routing rules?
  • How do you page my on-call tech, and what is the escalation if they do not respond within 10 minutes?
  • Do you integrate directly with my scheduling software, or do you send me a text and I enter it manually?
  • What are your holiday and weekend surcharge rates, in writing?
  • Is there a monthly minimum, and what is the overage rate?
  • Is the contract month to month, or a longer commitment?
  • Can I get call recordings or transcripts for quality review?
  • How long does onboarding take, and do I approve the script before you go live?

If a vendor pushes back on sharing their holiday rate sheet before you sign, that is a reason to look elsewhere.

Three mistakes that cost HVAC owners jobs

1. No written emergency rules. If you do not give the vendor a clear written list of what counts as an emergency, they page your tech for every call. You burn out your on-call tech, they start ignoring pages, and you lose the jobs you paid to cover. Write the rule before you sign: no heat below freezing is an emergency; a system blowing warm air in September is not.

2. Using a message service and calling it dispatch. A message service texts you a name and number. A dispatch service calls your tech. If your vendor sends a text at 11 PM and you check it at 7 AM, you do not have after-hours coverage. You have after-hours documentation of calls you lost.

3. Not testing before peak season. Set up in October, before the first cold snap. Call your own number at 10 PM and act like a caller with no heat. Check whether the agent follows your rules. Many owners set up service in late fall and discover in January that the agent has been booking no-heat calls as next-morning routine appointments.

Quick answers

Does the same service work for plumbing, garage door, and other trades?

Yes. Plumbing shops use it for leaks, frozen pipes, and water heater failures. Garage door companies use it for broken springs and stuck doors, where same-day urgency is similar to HVAC. Carpet cleaning and window cleaning shops use it mainly for lead capture and rebooking rather than emergency dispatch.

Can an AI answering service handle HVAC emergencies?

AI services can follow a triage script and send your on-call tech a text or alert. For standard no-heat and no-cool calls that follow a clear pattern, many HVAC owners find AI services sufficient. For calls with unclear situations or safety concerns, a live agent can probe further. Write your emergency rules tightly either way.

What if my on-call tech does not answer the page?

Ask your vendor about escalation before you sign. A good dispatch service tries your tech, waits 10 minutes, then moves to a second number. Write your escalation chain into your script: Tech 1, then Tech 2, then you. Without a written escalation, the agent has nowhere to go after the first missed call.

How long does it take to get set up?

contactcenterusa.com reports that a full setup including script approval and software integration typically takes about seven business days. The main delay is usually the owner not having emergency rules written down or their scheduling software login ready for the vendor.
