Answering Service Cost per Month: 2026 Pricing Breakdown

Last checked Nov 25, 2026. Prices and features change; check each provider.

Most home service businesses pay $50 to $250 a month for an AI answering service or $130 to $400 a month for live operators, based on published 2026 rates. Your actual number depends on call volume, call length, and the pricing model you pick. This guide breaks down current vendor prices, the fees that inflate your bill, and a simple way to estimate your real cost.

What answering services cost right now

Prices fall into two buckets: live (human) answering services and AI answering services. Live services cost more per minute but handle complex or emotional calls. AI services cost less and usually run 24/7 but may struggle with unusual requests.

What drives the price up

Pricing model. Services charge per minute, per call, or a flat monthly fee. Per-minute billing penalizes long calls. Per-call billing penalizes high volume with short calls. Flat-rate plans cap your minutes and charge overages above that cap.

Hours of coverage. Some live services charge $0.20 to $0.30 extra per call outside business hours (Upfirst, Nov 2026). Most AI services include 24/7 coverage at no extra charge.

Hidden fees to watch for:

  • Setup fees: $49 to $99 at some providers (Nextiva, Nov 2026)
  • Call rounding: some providers round a 63-second call to two full minutes, which inflates your bill fast (Upfirst, Nov 2026)
  • Appointment booking surcharges: $1.50 per booking at Smith.ai (GetNextPhone, Nov 2026)
  • Spam and wrong numbers: not every provider excludes these from your billable minutes

Questions to ask before you sign

Copy this list and send it to any answering service you are comparing. The answers will tell you more than the sticker price.

  • How do you bill: per minute, per call, or flat rate?
  • What is the overage rate if I go past my plan?
  • Do you round call times up? To the nearest 30 seconds or full minute?
  • Are spam calls and wrong numbers excluded from billable minutes?
  • Is there a setup fee or contract minimum?
  • Do after-hours or weekend calls cost extra?
  • Can you book appointments directly into my scheduling software?
  • How do you handle emergency calls like a gas leak or burst pipe?
  • Can I listen to call recordings or read transcripts?
  • What is the cancellation policy and notice period?

Calculate your real monthly cost

Sticker prices are meaningless if you blow past the included minutes every month. Use this method to compare providers on equal footing.

Example

You run a home service shop and get about 80 calls a month. Average call length is 3 minutes. That is 240 total minutes of answering time.

Provider A (live, per-minute): $179 base for 125 included minutes, $1.75 per extra minute. Your cost: $179 + (115 extra minutes x $1.75) = $380.25 per month.

Provider B (AI, flat rate): $99 base for 200 included minutes, $0.49 per extra minute. Your cost: $99 + (40 extra minutes x $0.49) = $118.60 per month.

Your estimate: (your monthly calls x your average minutes per call) = total minutes needed. Compare that number to each plan's included minutes. Multiply your overage minutes by the overage rate and add it to the base price. That is your real monthly cost.

If you do not know your call volume, check your phone bill or ask your phone provider for a call log from the last 90 days. Most cell carriers and VoIP services show call counts and durations in their online dashboard.

Quick answers

Is an AI answering service good enough for a home service business?

For taking messages, routing calls, and booking standard appointments, most AI services handle the job and cost a fraction of live operators. Where they fall short is handling upset customers, complex scheduling changes, or emergencies where the caller needs reassurance from a real person.

What is the biggest hidden cost in answering service billing?

Call rounding. If your provider rounds every call up to the next full minute, a 61-second call bills as two minutes. Over dozens of calls a month, that adds 20% to 40% to your real cost compared to what the base plan suggests.

Should I pick per-minute or per-call billing?

If your calls average under two minutes, per-minute billing usually costs less. If callers tend to describe problems in detail and calls run three minutes or longer, per-call billing caps your risk on each call. Track your actual call durations for a week before deciding.
